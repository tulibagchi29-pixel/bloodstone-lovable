# Bloodstone: A Novel

New landing page for my new book Bloodstone: 

“India’s answer to The Three-Body Problem”

Just as Liu Cixin’s The Three-Body Problem fused hard physics with Chinese historical trauma and philosophical scale — and found a second life as a visually stunning graphic novel by Wu Qingsong, blending classical Chinese painting technique with cosmic science-fiction imagery — Bloodstone, by Frankfurt-based author Shamit Bagchi, fuses real mathematics (the Monster group, Monstrous Moonshine) with Tantric cosmology and 7th-century Assamese history, brought to visual life through nearly 50 original illustrations by Kolkata-based artist Charbak Dipta.

The parallel, point by point:

Science as narrative engine, not decoration. Where Liu Cixin used real astrophysics and game theory to structure Three-Body‘s plot, Bagchi — who holds a master’s in Applied Physics alongside an MBA, and works professionally in AI strategy — uses the actual 196,883-dimensional representation of the Monster group as literal cosmological architecture in Bloodstone, not metaphor, but structure.

Illustration as a second narrative layer. Wu Qingsong translated Three-Body‘s abstract science and VR-game surrealism into striking sequential art rooted in Chinese painting tradition. Dipta does the equivalent for Bloodstone, rendering Kamakhya’s Tantric visual language and the novel’s mathematical cosmology through a comparable classical-meets-contemporary Indian aesthetic.

A national epic wearing genre clothes. Just as Three-Body is inseparable from Chinese 20th-century history, Bloodstone is inseparable from a specific Indian historical and spiritual lineage — Kamarupa, Kamakhya, Assamese Tantra — treated by Bagchi with the same rigor Liu Cixin gave Chinese history.

Global potential. Three-Body’s illustrated edition moved from Yilin Press in China to German and Spanish markets. Bloodstone, launching via Notion Press with a comparable author-illustrator identity, has the same cross-market shape: a book whose visual and conceptual hook travels independent of language.

One-line pitch: If The Three-Body Problem proved that hard science and national mythology could fuse into a global phenomenon, Bloodstone — written by physicist-turned-novelist Shamit Bagchi and illustrated by Charbak Dipta — attempts the same alchemy for India, pairing real mathematics with Tantric cosmology.
